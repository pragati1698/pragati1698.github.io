Case study 02 / Enterprise delivery

Bringing AI into workflows people already use.

Enterprise AI is not just model integration. It is the work of connecting real information sources, handling access and governance constraints, and fitting into the systems employees already use.

The problem

Employees often need information scattered across knowledge pages, documents and existing tools. Some questions can be answered from trusted content; others should become structured requests routed to the appropriate support channel.

A useful AI assistant must handle both paths without inventing answers or creating new work for the teams it is supposed to help.

Successful deployment begins with the employee workflow — not with the model.

From stakeholder need to implementation

In my software engineering and applied AI work at the Connecticut Department of Transportation, I have developed Copilot Studio agent solutions and supporting automation for enterprise knowledge and IT intake scenarios.

Engineering tradeoffs

Integration before reinvention

Using platforms employees already know can reduce adoption friction and the amount of custom application code required, while introducing platform governance and licensing constraints.

Helpful answers need grounding

The assistant should prioritize supported enterprise knowledge and clearly handle questions the available data cannot answer. Retrieval quality and access permissions must be tested separately.

Publishing is not the same as approval

Technical enablement, security review, stakeholder testing and production authorization are distinct steps. The portfolio describes the implementation and rollout work without presenting unapproved functionality as a completed public launch.

What this demonstrates

My experience covers the delivery side of applied AI: clarifying requirements, evaluating platforms, connecting enterprise sources, implementing agent behavior, designing intake/automation steps, troubleshooting deployment constraints, and coordinating with business and technical stakeholders.

Those are the core activities I want to bring to forward-deployed AI engineering: own the problem, make architectural tradeoffs, build the integration, and improve the result with users.

What comes next

Measure task-completion rate, grounded-answer quality, unsupported-response behavior, access-control boundaries, latency, and the number of requests reaching the correct team. Any production adoption or impact figures should only be published after validation and employer approval.
